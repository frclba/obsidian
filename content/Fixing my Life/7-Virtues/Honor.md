[[The Seven Bushido Virtues]]

Honor is your reputation with yourself. Not what others think of you — what *you* think of you when no one's watching.

In Bushido, honor (*Meiyo*) meant that your actions reflected your deepest values at all times. A samurai who acted dishonorably, even in secret, had lost something more valuable than any battle.

The modern world is confusing about honor. Sometimes it gets mixed up with ego, with saving face, with pride. But real honor is quieter than that. It's:
- Keeping your commitments even when it's inconvenient
- Admitting mistakes instead of covering them up
- Treating people with dignity even when they can't do anything for you
- Refusing to compromise your values for short-term gain

Honor is the bridge between [[Honesty]] and [[Loyalty]]. You're honest because your honor demands it. You're loyal because your honor won't let you abandon those who count on you.

The thing about honor is that it's built slowly and destroyed quickly. Every small choice matters. Every time you cut a corner, skip a commitment, or say one thing and do another, you chip away at it.

But here's the good news: honor is entirely within your control. You can't control outcomes, other people's opinions, or what life throws at you. But you can always control whether you act with integrity.
